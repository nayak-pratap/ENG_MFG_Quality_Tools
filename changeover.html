What it does to batch size

Changeovers per day = budget divided by changeover time; batch = demand divided by changeovers per day. This is the link between setup reduction and flow: smaller setups buy smaller batches at the same cost in lost time, which is what shortens lead time.

Method. Internal tasks need the machine stopped; external tasks can be done while it runs. The changeover time a line feels is the internal total. Stage one separates the two and moves out everything that can be external, which usually needs organisation rather than money. Stage two streamlines what is left, which is where tooling and fixtures earn their cost. Published case figures for the first stage alone cluster around 60%, but almost all of them come from consultancies, are self-reported and carry no baselines, so treat them as illustrative and measure your own. Two cautions from practitioners: do not apply this everywhere, use the constraint or OEE data to choose where it pays; and expect the gains to drift back unless the new sequence becomes standard work that someone re-checks.
